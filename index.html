02 About

Where theory meets
the workbench.

Student Innovation Lab bridges the gap between classroom learning and real-world engineering. Here, students don't just study technology—they build it, break it, and make it better.

Our interdisciplinary space brings together students from across engineering and design to work on projects that span robotics, intelligent systems, control theory, and human–computer interaction. Faculty mentors guide the work, but students drive it.

Learn by Building

Students tackle real engineering problems from day one—assembling robots, training models, and wiring circuits instead of just reading about them.

Research Through Experimentation

Every idea gets tested. The lab provides equipment, compute, and mentorship so students can run experiments that challenge assumptions.

Innovate Together

Mechanical engineers sit next to computer scientists. Designers collaborate with control theorists. The best work happens at disciplinary boundaries.

03 Facilities

Spaces designed for making.

Five interconnected zones support the full cycle of engineering work—from brainstorming and simulation to physical prototyping and testing. Descriptions below are representative; check with the lab for current equipment.

FAC_01

Robotics & Automation Zone

Robot prototyping, sensing, actuation, and autonomous systems. Equipped with manipulators, mobile platforms, and a motion-capture area.

FAC_02

Control Systems Bench

Feedback control, system modeling, and real-time experimentation with physical plants and digital controllers.

FAC_03

AI & Computing Workspace

Machine learning, computer vision, and intelligent application development. GPU workstations and shared compute resources.

FAC_04

HCI & Interaction Studio

Interface prototyping, usability exploration, and interactive device development. Includes display walls, input devices, and a user-testing area.

FAC_05

Electronics & Prototyping Area

Embedded systems, circuit development, and rapid fabrication. Soldering stations, oscilloscopes, 3D printers, and a PCB mill.

* Facility descriptions are illustrative. Contact the lab for current equipment and availability.
